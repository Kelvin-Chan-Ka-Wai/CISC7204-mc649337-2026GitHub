## Reflection (~200 words)

Building this portfolio showed me that the hard part of a data-science deliverable is not the
plot, it is making the plot checkable. I can take a raw CSV, clean and aggregate it with pandas,
and turn it into an interactive Bokeh figure with hover tooltips and a colour mapping — but what
this project actually taught me is to state the unit on the axis, annotate the number the claim
depends on, and write down the limitation before someone else finds it. Three things demonstrate
that: a population chart that shows convergence rather than just two lines, a price chart where
the median is drawn against the mean so the skew is visible, and an Olympic scatter where the
correlation is in the title and the three countries I name are labelled on the plot. I also
learned to treat reproducibility as part of the work: the notebooks are pinned, they run in CI on
every push, and the figures are generated by a script rather than exported by hand. My specific
next improvement is to add a regression with confidence intervals to the Olympic analysis, so
that r = 0.87 arrives with an uncertainty range instead of standing alone as a bare number.

*(205 words)*

## Responsible sharing check

- No passwords, API keys, tokens, private keys or personal contact details in either repository
  or on the site.
- The three datasets are public and their sources and permissions are recorded in `data/README.md`:
  World Bank WDI CC BY 4.0 with additional terms, UCI Automobile CC BY 4.0, and the public Rio
  2016 athlete source. No explicit upstream redistribution licence was found for the Olympic
  file, so it is retained for course reproducibility rather than relicensed.
- No restricted or licensed third-party data is redistributed; the 50 MB `stock_prices.csv` from
  the tutorial is deliberately excluded.
- The population and car analyses are aggregated. The Olympic source contains individual public
  athlete records (name, sex, date of birth, height and weight), not anonymised data; only those
  public fields are retained and the analysis aggregates them by country.
